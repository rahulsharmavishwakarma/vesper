# Vesper motion and interaction audit

Updated 18 September 2026. Apple recommends purposeful, brief feedback that follows the interaction people performed. This supports a restrained Studio interface with clear responses to clicks, selections and recording state. [Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

The timings below are **Vesper design choices**, not durations prescribed by Apple or accessibility standards.

## Brand motion

The Ember lockup is ambient in the sidebar: the two call dashes breathe on a
2.6 s chirp (350 ms stagger on the second) and the eye-lid highlight blinks on a
4.2 s cycle, both `ease-in-out`. Hovering tilts the bird −8° with a spring
(`cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)`). The bird body and wordmark never move; this is
not a pulse or recording indicator. Opening About replays the idle motion, and
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` stills the chirp, blink and tilt with the
complete lockup visible. The sheet retains its existing independent entrance
and focus behavior.
The shared component consumes the Ember artwork described in
[UI design](ui-design.md#selected-identity). The Songbird entrance it replaces
(two 480 ms call-stroke reveals) is preserved in history in
[issue #71](https://github.com/rahulsharmavishwakarma/transcribe/issues/71).

**Ember sidebar icons and 3 / Quiet creator labels are selected and integrated.**
The exploratory comparisons under `docs/design/` remain local and are excluded
from source control. Production uses the application's navigation glyphs and
plain creator names; it bundles none of the reference NVIDIA, Cohere or Qwen logo
assets. Creator identity follows original model provenance rather than runtime,
hardware backend or community publisher. The production motion and accessibility

The waveform implements the user's **W02 Reed** selection: 45 rounded strokes in Dictate and all six compact capture designs, with compact thickness fitted to the available width. It is a stylized display of measured amplitude and estimated fundamental pitch, not a spectrum or recorded PCM. The tapered envelope uses DC-free RMS and 60–700 Hz pitch estimates. The measured-pitch display hold expires after 160 ms without a valid estimate. Visual spacing remains at its last accepted shape through unvoiced sound, without reporting a new pitch; it resets to neutral after silence or a recording/visibility reset. Measured amplitude responds independently. A jump of at least 0.75 octave must persist for 60 ms while the prior estimate remains valid. This rejects isolated outliers without asserting a new measured pitch. Every lobe follows the supplied pitch; only processing uses phase motion. Silence cannot invent speech. Processing uses an explicitly indeterminate pattern with the operation label. Reduced motion freezes geometry and retains measured brightness and readable state labels. Polling and drawing stop when hidden or unmounted; late responses cannot revive an old recording. Bridge readings expire 200 ms from the request start; replies that take 200 ms or more are discarded. The capture worker also clears telemetry if no fresh PCM arrives for 200 ms. Fast replies do not carry a native sample timestamp, so freshness within that backend interval remains unmeasured. Dictate and quick-capture browser previews do not invent microphone input. The separate Settings design sample uses explicitly labeled simulated input only to demonstrate the selected layout.

An explicit refresh rotates its icon while the request is outstanding and gives the returned bars a short entrance. Displayed measurements never interpolate through invented numbers, and automatic sampling does not continuously replay the refresh animation.

H02 stores an optional `transcriptFeedback` (`like`, `dislike`, or null) in the existing session JSON. Older records default to null. The UI moves immediately with a “Saving feedback…” status, disables repeated writes, and restores the persisted value on failure. Selecting the same thumb clears it; Undo saves the previous choice. A rating neither approves a training label nor changes personalization. History refresh/reopening restores the saved value; no new store, migration, inference lifetime, or training action is introduced.

The capture callback meters the latest 40 ms of samples through a preallocated rolling AC RMS window. Each channel has separate running sums and squared sums; subtracting its mean energy excludes DC offsets, including opposite offsets in stereo. Unlike per-callback RMS, its duration is independent of callback size at the same sample endpoint. It allocates and locks nothing during capture and preserves the original PCM. This bounded averaging adds temporal smoothing; it does not guarantee that every short transient survives slow device callbacks or delayed GUI polling.

Pitch analysis runs on the existing capture worker after draining the callback buffer, at most once every 40 ms. It applies a bounded FIR equivalent to four cascaded stride-length box filters before downsampling into a disposable 512-sample mono view at roughly 8 kHz (about 58–64 ms at common rates, plus less than 0.4 ms of filter support). This suppresses high-frequency folding into the voice range. Rates outside 8–384 kHz or channel counts outside 1–32 return no pitch. Archived PCM and ASR chunks are preserved. Each lag comparison is anchored to the newest half-window, so fresh audio is used instead of leaving the final portion of the buffer outside the calculation. A YIN-style normalized difference function selects the first reliable trough and interpolates its period; a 0.15 threshold rejects weak periodicity and a separate AC-energy gate rejects silence/DC. This is a bounded UI estimator, without YIN's full trajectory refinement. Its method follows de Cheveigne and Kawahara's 2002 work, also documented in the [official aubio YIN implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aubio/aubio/master/src/pitch/pitchyin.c). The code is an independent Rust implementation with no new dependency. The range, gates and visual mapping are application choices. It is not a calibrated pitch instrument: breath, noise, multiple voices, strong harmonics and out-of-range sounds can have no reliable estimate or produce octave errors. Synthetic tone tests establish the tested frequency/rate/amplitude cases only; they do not establish accuracy on live speech.

The recorder omits repeated readiness, microphone-detected and wait instructions. Normal capture shows the waveform, elapsed time and controls; actionable setup, quiet-input and failure messages remain visible. Saved-result copy is concise, and training-review guidance stays in History.

Tab entrances finish before pointer, focus or assistive click activation measures a control. `entranceMotion` finishes only the named finite entrance animations; it leaves microphone and press feedback alone. Finishing the animation rather than removing it in a `:focus-within` rule prevents an unwanted replay on blur. Menus retain their own brief opening motion, and their anchor positions stay fixed. Completed entrances leave no transform on the section. Ordinary runtime/data updates do not restart the graph reveal. Reduced motion immediately shows complete sections and chart paths, including during a preference change.

The quick-capture behavior was informed by the common start/stop shortcut and compact recording feedback documented by [Superwhisper](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/settings-shortcuts), while its layout, wording, signal rail, colors, typography, and motion are original to Vesper. It uses Tauri's official [global shortcut](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/global-shortcut/), [notification](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/notification/), and [window configuration](https://v2.tauri.app/reference/config/#windowconfig) APIs. Notifications report only completion or failure and omit transcript content.

Reduced motion must cover both CSS **and JavaScript/Web Animations API** effects. Read `prefers-reduced-motion` at activation, cancel any ongoing nonessential movement if the preference changes, and retain static state labels and visible focus. WCAG’s interaction-animation criterion permits disabling nonessential interaction motion; MDN documents the platform preference used to implement it. [WAI: Animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html), [MDN: Reduced motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)

Acceptance checks: mouse, touch, Enter and Space produce equivalent acknowledgement; disabled controls stay inert; pointer cancellation and blur release pressed state; rapid clicks never leave a transform stuck; focus appears immediately; reduced motion works while recording; switching tabs preserves jobs and drafts. Check Record, red Stop, selected menu options and focus outlines against their actual backgrounds. Necessary non-text control/state indicators need 3:1 contrast against adjacent colors; decorative bars do not replace readable status. [WAI: Non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)

The refinement follows [web.dev's guidance on transform/opacity animation](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide) and [reduced-motion preferences](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion). Form focus, selected navigation icons, filter selections, and dropdown checks receive short feedback; meter, focus, and recording actions never wait for those animations. The requested 40 ms sampling interval is a scheduling choice, not a measured end-to-end microphone latency guarantee.

Research separates pitch validity from amplitude and time scheduling. The [pYIN paper](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2014/MauchDixon-PYIN-ICASSP2014.pdf) models pitch and voiced/unvoiced transitions separately; [Praat's official pitch guidance](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/manual/pitch_analysis_by_filtered_autocorrelation.html) likewise distinguishes octave jumps from voicing transitions. These motivate a bounded display hold, not a claim that this repository implements pYIN or inherits its accuracy. The 160 ms hold and 60 ms confirmation are application choices: large sustained pitch changes can incur about 60–100 ms confirmation at ordinary polling cadence, in addition to the estimator and 35 ms visual smoothing. After the previous estimate expires, reacquisition is immediate.

[CPAL 0.16.0](https://docs.rs/cpal/0.16.0/cpal/struct.InputStreamTimestamp.html) supplies callback/capture timestamps, but its [macOS implementation](https://github.com/RustAudio/cpal/blob/v0.16.0/src/host/coreaudio/macos/mod.rs#L552-L565) estimates capture time from callback time and buffer duration. Those fields would not independently measure physical ADC latency. [Browser timers](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/timers-and-user-prompts.html#timers) are not exact clocks, and [Tauri's event documentation](https://v2.tauri.app/develop/calling-frontend/#event-system) does not promise a low-latency event transport. The existing single-flight command remains; this fix avoids adding roundtrip time to every interval and prevents expired responses from reviving the display.
