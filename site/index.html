Speak. It’s written.

Vesper turns your voice into text on your Mac. Hold a shortcut, talk, and your words appear in the app you’re typing in.

Download for Mac View on GitHub

For Macs with Apple silicon. Open source, and in early development.

Settled and still listening

Tidy it, or keep it as said.

Turn on writing cleanup and a small writing model, Qwen3.5 0.8B, works on your Mac. As I said it adds punctuation and capitals. Polished also drops fillers and fixes what was misheard. Your original transcript is always kept.

It learns your names.

Teach Vesper the names and terms you use, like Arclight or Priya. It also learns from the corrections you make, and keeps what it learns on this Mac only.

Setup picks a model for you

When you set up Vesper, it recommends the best speech model for your language and your Mac, based on published benchmarks. You can change it any time.

  • NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3
  • Cohere Transcribe
  • Qwen3-ASR 0.6B
  • NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming
  • IBM Granite Speech
  • OpenAI Whisper
  • Moonshine

Quick on Apple silicon.

Vesper runs the speech model on your Mac’s GPU, and on the Neural Engine for the models that use it. It chooses on its own, so there are no settings to tune.

    It stays on your Mac.

    Speech and text are processed on your Mac, and your recordings stay there. There’s no account to create and no cloud API key to paste in.

    Recordings, too.

    Import an audio file to get a transcript. Your recordings and their text are saved to History, where you can search, review, copy and export them.

    Opening it the first time

    Vesper isn’t notarized yet, so macOS shows a warning on first launch. To open it:

    1. Open System Settings.
    2. Go to Privacy & Security.
    3. Choose Open Anyway.
